6 July – 13 July 2026

BLUF — Bottom Line Up Front

Across five global theatres, military conflicts are shifting toward structural manipulation, deep precision strikes, and grey-zone operations to force irreversible ground realities. In Ukraine, Russia is exploiting Ukraine's severe advanced interceptor shortage by launching smaller, highly effective ballistic missile salvos that bypass air defences, emphasising a critical global requirement to abandon "just-in-time" supply chains in favour of rapid-production, layered architectures. Concurrently, in the Middle East, US-brokered LAF "Pilot Zones" are paralysed by Hezbollah civil war threats; Hamas has dissolved its civil government to shift humanitarian liabilities onto the US-chaired peace framework. This forces an unintended, combat-power-siphoning mission creep onto ground forces, who must act as de facto city managers while Hamas preserves its underground military assets to transition into a pure shadow insurgency. Following an 11 July Iranian IRGC strike on commercial shipping and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, CENTCOM retaliated on 12 July. Overnight into 13 July, Iran launched a massive, multi-axis ballistic missile and drone counter-offensive targeting US infrastructure across Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, and Jordan.

Key Developments

Theatre 01

Russia – Ukraine

Smaller Ballistic Salvos Exploit Interceptor Shortage
Fires & Strikes
Russia's exploitation of Ukraine's anti-ballistic missile interceptor deficits

Russian Aerospace Forces (VKS) adjusted their strike methodology to capitalise on a critical Ukrainian vulnerability: a severe deficit in anti-ballistic missile interceptors (such as PAC-3 Patriot systems). During a large-scale strike package launched overnight on 5–6 July, Russian forces deployed a mix of 351 attack drones and 68 missiles. While Ukrainian air defences successfully intercepted nearly all sub-sonic cruise missiles (like the Kh-101) and Shahed-type drones, none of the 29 launched ballistic missiles (comprising Iskander-M and S-400 systems) were shot down. Operational data indicates that the VKS is intentionally decreasing the total volume of ballistic missiles per strike package. This shift leverages the reality that mass saturation is no longer required to penetrate and overwhelm Ukraine's depleted tactical air defence umbrella. Highlighting this defensive gap, the 6 July strike caused severe kinetic damage across the Kyiv region. In the Kyiv suburb of Vyshneve, ballistic impacts severely damaged hundreds of homes and forced the evacuation of roughly 600 residents due to the hazard of unexploded ordnance.iswopb

Implication [Protection]
  • Russia is now getting missiles through with smaller salvos, not big ones. That is because Ukraine's expensive interceptors have run low. Any modern force should plan for the same problem: expensive missile defence runs out fast. Keep the top-end interceptors for critical targets only, and back them up with cheaper, faster-to-build interceptors for everything else. Ukraine's fast-tracked FREYA project (European radars, local missiles) is one working example.
  • A single Patriot interceptor takes 24–30 months to build because of specialised sub-parts like solid rocket motors. If a war starts, you cannot buy them fast enough. Ukraine secured a US production licence at political level this week — but you have to lock in the licences and stockpile the parts years in advance. Just-in-time supply chains do not work for missile defence.
Theatre 02

Israel – Lebanon

Terrain-Denial Demolitions & Pilot Zone Paralysis
Manoeuvre
Targeted neutralisation and border hardening

While a framework agreement exists on paper to outline a phased Israeli withdrawal, the reality on the ground remains intensely kinetic. Israel has adopted a strict doctrine of immediate, proactive kinetic response to clear out what it assesses as Hezbollah's attempt to reconstitute forces south of the Litani River. Throughout the week, the Israeli Air Force (IAF) and drone platforms launched continuous precision strikes in southern towns like Nabatieh, al-Mansouri, and Kfar Kila. Tactically, Israel is not just engaging targets; it is structurally modifying the terrain. In border towns like Houla and Khiam, IDF engineering and ground units carried out systematic, controlled demolitions of residential and built-up structures. The operational intent is to permanently deny Hezbollah the physical infrastructure needed for forward observation, ATGM launch positions, or subterranean caching near the Blue Line.

Stabilisation
US military mission & the "Pilot Zone" concept

Concurrently, a major Western diplomatic–military push crystallised in Beirut. On 11 July, a high-level US military delegation met with the LAF command to finalise the mechanics of the "Pilot Zone". The Trilateral Framework aims to have the IDF gradually cede territory to the Lebanese Armed Forces, rather than directly to international peacekeepers, to prevent non-state actors from re-entering. The initial pilot zones focus on highly symbolic and tactical ground, including the Beaufort Ridge area, Zoutar al-Sharqiya, and Yahmar. Politically and tactically, Hezbollah has fiercely resisted this deployment. Secretary-General Naim Qassem explicitly declared the framework illegitimate, while field operatives have subtly repositioned assets. Hezbollah is actively warning the LAF that executing disarmament or barring them from the south will trigger a domestic civil war — using political coercion to paralyse the LAF's willingness to secure the pilot zones.anadoluisw

Implication [Civil-Military · Force Design]
  • Blowing up buildings to deny cover works tactically but comes with a bill. Civilian displacement, war-crimes allegations (Amnesty International issued a report on 9 July), and years of hostility from the local population. Any force imposing a buffer zone through demolitions has to pay that bill eventually.
  • Getting a proxy army (the LAF here) to hold ground against an armed group inside its own country (Hezbollah) is fragile. If the proxy is threatened with civil war for enforcing the deal, it stops enforcing. The original force is then stuck either occupying long-term or watching the ground revert to the adversary. Do not assume a proxy force will do the hard part of a ceasefire on its own.
Theatre 03

Israel – Gaza

Hamas Dissolves Government to Shift Costs
Strategic Intent
High-stakes asymmetric political manoeuvre

On 6 July, Hamas announced the complete resignation and dissolution of its civil administration. Hamas's sudden dissolution of its civil administration is a calculated asymmetric manoeuvre designed to shed the unsustainable financial and political burden of governing a devastated Gaza Strip while shifting the immediate humanitarian and administrative liability onto the unequipped, US-chaired Board of Peace (BoP). By decoupling its vulnerable public governance profile from its core militant structure, Hamas aims to preserve its underground military assets, avoid local blame for systemic infrastructure collapse, and weaponise the international peace framework. Ultimately, this structural retreat allows Hamas to transition into a pure shadow insurgency, positioning its embedded personnel to quietly infiltrate the new, internationally funded administrative apparatus while retaining shadow control over the local population.cnnrohangunaratna

Implication [Sustainment · Civil-Military]
  • If the local government stops functioning and no peacekeeping force has arrived, whoever holds the ground has to keep the population alive — water, sewage, food, hospitals, garbage. Combat units end up doing this work.
  • That is the point of Hamas dissolving its government. It shifts the cost of running Gaza (paying salaries, restoring services, absorbing anger about failures) onto whoever tries to replace them. Meanwhile Hamas keeps its fighters, tunnels, and weapons intact, and waits.
  • For any force considering a stabilisation mission: budget the humanitarian workload before deployment, not after. Combat units are the worst tool for humanitarian delivery — expensive, slow, poorly trained for it — but they end up doing it when the civil side is not ready. Get the civil side ready first, or expect the mission to drag on.
Theatre 04

Israel – US – Iran

Hormuz Closure Triggers Multi-Axis Iranian Counter-Offensive
Fires & Strikes
Iran strikes commercial shipping, closes Hormuz, hits US bases across five states

On 11 July, Iran's IRGC shattered a fragile truce by striking a commercial ship and declaring the Strait of Hormuz completely closed to maritime traffic. The US immediately nullified the ceasefire, launching massive air and cruise missile strikes on 12 July against Iranian coastal defences and naval bases. Rather than backing down, Iran launched a coordinated, multi-axis ballistic missile and drone counter-offensive overnight into 13 July, targeting US military infrastructure across Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, and Oman. Most critically, Iranian missiles bypassed air defence networks to strike key fuel and ammunition depots at Prince Hassan Air Base in Jordan, drastically expanding the kinetic geography of the conflict.straitstimesaljazeera

Implication [Protection · C4 Resilience]
  • Iran hit fuel and ammunition dumps at Prince Hassan Air Base in Jordan. Jordan is not the "front line" — it is supposed to be rear-area, safer than Bahrain or Qatar. Iran's ballistic missiles have the range to hit rear-area bases too. There is no safe distance inside the theatre. Do not concentrate fuel or ammunition at any base.
  • The fix is layered: active defences (Patriot, THAAD) in more layers, plus physical hardening of the base itself — buried fuel storage, spread-out ammunition, spare runways, fast-repair crews. Passive protection is not optional.
  • Iranian strikes and jamming knocked out most radars, early-warning systems, and civilian aviation tracking across the Gulf. Everyone lost the "big picture" for hours. If a unit relies on theatre-wide data links, plan for those going dark. Train units to fight with only their own radar, their own drone feed, and their own scouts — and rehearse it, so the loss of the wider network does not stop the unit.
Theatre 05

Thailand – Cambodia

Engineering Encroachment & Frontier Hardening
Combat EngineeringGrey Zone
RTA engineering encroachment past Boundary Pillars 14 and 22

Despite the 27 December 2025 ceasefire, the Royal Thai Army (RTA) aggressively executed a physical fait accompli strategy to permanently fortify high-ground positions seized in late 2025. Between 6 and 7 July, Thai engineering units pushed past recognised border lines at Boundary Pillar No. 14 (O'Smach commune) to construct permanent defensive embankments and deep razor-wire barriers. Pushing this footprint westward on 9 July, RTA forces crossed the frontier at Boundary Pillar No. 22 (Thmor Daun village) to install additional concrete culvert barricades, effectively utilising combat engineers as non-kinetic instruments to structurally reshape the forward line of own troops (FLOT).thestar

Implication [Grey Zone · Combat Engineering]
  • Thailand is doing two things at once: participating in the legal case (UNCLOS conciliation) while pushing barbed wire, embankments, and concrete culverts onto disputed ground. The lawsuit manages the international pressure; the engineering creates a new border.
  • The lesson: when an adversary opens a legal case, watch the ground — that is where the real move is happening. Legal proceedings can slow the international response long enough for permanent physical changes to become "the way it has always been".
