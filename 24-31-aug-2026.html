BLUF — Bottom Line Up Front

Across active global flashpoints, military operations are increasingly characterised by high-tech industrial attrition and multi-vector precision strikes. In the Russia–Ukraine war, deep-rear infrastructure attacks have crippled domestic fuel and food-logistics networks on both sides. In the Middle East, Hezbollah’s drone usage along the Ali al-Taher Ridge and the widening Iran–US–Israel kinetic exchange — featuring US strikes in the Strait of Hormuz and retaliatory ballistic salvos hitting Jordan and the UAE — demonstrate the acute vulnerability of regional transit hubs and static outposts to low-cost uncrewed systems and standoff fires.

Key Developments

Theatre 01

Russia – Ukraine

Refinery Strike Campaign & Collapse of Food-Logistics Networks
Strategic Operations
Operational rear-area interdiction & strategic attrition

Ukrainian forces intensified long-range strike operations targeting Russian energy infrastructure and deep-rear strike-generation nodes — striking the Syzran refinery in Samara Oblast (23–24 August), the Lukoil-NORSI refinery in Kstovo (26 August, taking all major Lukoil facilities offline), and the Kirishi/KINEF refinery in Leningrad Oblast on the night of 29–30 August — Russia’s second-largest refinery. Russia has suspended all gasoline exports until 30 September to ease the domestic fuel crisis caused by Ukrainian strikes. The operational objective focused on degrading Russian logistical endurance and forcing the Kremlin to negotiate before winter.

Concurrently, Russian forces executed coordinated large-scale drone and missile salvos targeting Ukrainian energy grids, industrial base, and military logistics hubs to degrade Ukraine’s strategic rear ahead of autumn/winter. Three consecutive days of near-constant strikes on Kyiv damaged residential buildings and retail logistics hubs; Ukraine’s Agriculture Minister acknowledged that roughly 90% of the food-logistics infrastructure of major retail chains has been destroyed. The conflict remains an operational war of industrial endurance, where deep precision strikes against force-generation infrastructure are progressively constraining each combatant’s capacity to sustain the war.yahookyivindependentkyivindependent

Implication

A high-tech, precision-capable force with finite inventories, the Army must balance its focus on high-end, high-cost precision munitions with scalable, cost-effective counter-drone capabilities and attrition-resistant stocks. Defence technology partnerships and local defence industrial capacity must focus on rapid manufacturing and repair capabilities. The Army must reinforce point-defence systems for critical nodes. Hardening infrastructure, redundancy, and air defence integration (SHORAD to medium-range) are vital to preventing rapid operational paralysis.

Theatre 02

Israel – Lebanon

Contested Operations on the Ali al-Taher Ridge
Fires & Strikes
Hezbollah one-way attack drones against consolidating forces

The dominant tactical action continued to centre on the strategic Ali al-Taher Ridge near Nabatieh — high ground that overlooks al-Nabatieh and towns in northern Israel, and beneath which Hezbollah maintains a major underground facility identified as a Badr Unit headquarters. Israeli forces continued operations to consolidate the ridge, reportedly having surrounded a number of Hezbollah fighters there. On the night of 26 August, Hezbollah launched two one-way explosive attack drones at IDF troops operating at the ridge; IDF troops fired at the UAVs and downed one, with no Israeli injuries reported. In response, on Thursday 27 August, the IDF executed a series of strikes across the Nabatieh area targeting Hezbollah infrastructure.acleddataynetnewsjnsarabnews

Implication

The deployment of low-altitude, explosive one-way attack drones against holding forces highlights the reliance on uncrewed aerial systems (UAS) to contest static or semi-static defensive outposts. Defence task forces and manoeuvre forces require fully integrated, multi-layered C-UAS defences down to the tactical section and platoon levels. Relying solely on small-arms fire or localised electronic warfare (EW) is insufficient. Forces operating at forward posts need kinetic hard-kill SHORAD (Short-Range Air Defence) integrated with dynamic soft-kill jamming to protect stationary troops and combat engineers during consolidation phases.

Theatre 03

Israel – Gaza

Target System Expansion in the Central & Southern Corridors
Strategic Operations
Strikes against critical utilities and dual-use hubs

Israeli forces executed multi-domain strikes across Gaza on 24 August targeting critical civilian infrastructure and shelter areas — including a water desalination plant in the Sheikh Radwan neighbourhood of Gaza City (alongside the adjacent al-Radwan Mosque prayer hall), an aid warehouse in Deir al-Balah, the Abu Salim mosque in Deir al-Balah, and the Askelon prayer hall in the Beach refugee camp. In the central and southern corridors, strikes on shelter areas killed at least seven Palestinians. Rather than large-scale armoured manoeuvres, the IDF maintained operational pressure through evacuation orders (Beach refugee camp and Deir al-Balah), air-to-ground precision strikes, artillery shelling of Al-Bureij and eastern Gaza City, tank fire toward displacement tents at Asdaa City and Hamad Towers, and naval fire off the Rafah coast. The IDF’s stated intent was to ‘thwart imminent attacks by Hamas and other Gaza militants’ and to target Hamas weapons storage sites (including two inside mosques). Al Jazeera’s weekly framing captured the operational shift: within hours of the 17 August Kushner–Netanyahu talks, ‘senior officers were describing a return to pre-ceasefire rules of engagement’.aljazeeraaljazeera

Implication

The expansion of strikes against critical utilities (water desalination) and dual-use hubs (warehouses, places of worship) reflects an operational concept designed to deny adversary force-regeneration and subterranean access within civilian enclaves. Army Intelligence and Fires organs must maintain high-resolution, real-time Target Intelligence Packages (TIP) capable of rapid reassessment under flexible ROEs. This highlights the operational necessity of precision strike assets — such as High Mobility Artillery Rocket Systems (HIMARS) and uncrewed aerial strike platforms — capable of executing low-collateral, highly surgical strikes in dense urban structures while neutralising time-sensitive threats.

Theatre 04

Israel – US – Iran

Hormuz Strike & IRGC Ballistic Retaliation on Jordan and the UAE
Fires & Strikes
Kinetic resumption: Strait of Hormuz strike & ballistic retaliation

On 30 August, US Central Command (CENTCOM) executed targeted precision strikes on two Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) rocket launchers on Larak Island, in the narrowest section of the Strait of Hormuz off the coast of Bandar Abbas. US Navy Capt. Tim Hawkins, CENTCOM spokesperson: ‘Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps forces were observed preparing to launch rockets with sea mines into the Strait of Hormuz.’ The strike occurred days after CENTCOM Commander Adm. Brad Cooper announced (approximately 28 August) that international shipping lanes had been cleared of Iranian sea mines — ‘a watershed moment’ — indicating IRGC forces had immediately begun repositioning new mine-delivery systems in response.

The IRGC responded within 24 hours. On 31 August, IRGC ballistic missiles struck the King Hussein and Al Azraq US bases in Jordan, while IRGC drones targeted Al Minhad Air Base in the United Arab Emirates. The Jordanian Armed Forces confirmed its air defence systems intercepted all eight ballistic missiles that entered Jordanian airspace, destroying them before they could pose a threat to people or property. Iran framed the retaliation as targeting ‘technical infrastructure and locations of military aircraft.’ The two-venue retaliation is analytically consequential — Iran chose to hit both a US-hosting ally (Jordan) and a Gulf state that had suspended trade with Iran on 19 August (UAE), signalling both direct US response and punitive action against Gulf compliance with US economic isolation.cnbcthenationalnews

Implication

Iran’s simultaneous retaliatory strikes using ballistic missiles against Jordan and uncrewed aerial systems (UAS) against the UAE highlight the routine use of multi-vector standoff fires targeting fixed military facilities. Combining low-radar-cross-section strike drones with high-speed theatre ballistic missiles demands seamless integration between SHORAD, GBAD, and C-UAS to safeguard critical force-generation nodes. Iran’s targeting of the UAE — partially motivated by diplomatic and trade suspensions — demonstrates that third-party nations and regional transit hubs face direct kinetic exposure during broader theatre conflicts. National infrastructure, fuel reserves, and supply networks must be hardened against indirect operational shockwaves. Supply chain resilience, emergency fuel stockpiling, and civilian emergency preparedness are essential components to maintain operational freedom of action despite global maritime interdiction.

Theatre 05

Thailand – Cambodia

Hill 500 Inspection Triggers Cartographic Lawfare Dispute
Strategic
Political-diplomatic friction over high-level border inspections

On 23 August 2026, Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul visited Thai troops at Hill 500 near the Chong An Ma crossing. Cambodia’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation lodged its strongest protest yet in a 25 August press release, condemning the visit as an "unauthorised" act constituting a "deliberate attempt to assert illegitimate claims over Cambodian territory... illegally occupied by Thai armed forces since last year". Cambodia bases its position on the 1:200,000-scale maps produced by the Franco-Siamese Boundary Delimitation Commissions and Point 1 of the Joint Statement of the 3rd Special Meeting of the Cambodia–Thailand General Border Committee (GBC) of 27 December 2025, arguing that "an unlawful act cannot generate lawful rights".

Thailand rejected the Cambodian protest across three arguments: (i) the area is Thai territory; (ii) the visit constituted a routine troop inspection which the December 2025 Joint Statement explicitly distinguishes from provocation; and (iii) boundary disputes should be resolved through the Joint Boundary Committee process, not political-level assertions. On Wednesday 27 August, Anutin publicly dismissed a Cambodian call for legal action against him, saying he had acted in accordance with Thai law and the bilateral Joint Statement.akpthethaigerkhaosodenglish

Implication

The Army must integrate legal, historic, and cartographic advisors directly into operational planning. Information Operations must be prepared to counter an adversary’s legalistic narratives ("lawfare") instantly with verified geospatial data, aerial surveillance, and clear operational messaging to maintain regional and international legitimacy.
