21 September – 28 September 2026

BLUF — Bottom Line Up Front

Across all theatres, operations have shifted decisively toward permanent area denial, subterranean denial, and multi-theatre A2/AD consolidation: Ukraine disrupted Russian infiltration corridors via UGV demining and EW interdiction; Israel fortified permanent security postures in Lebanon and Gaza to prevent force reconstitution; Iran and its proxies expanded a dual-chokepoint maritime and subterranean threat envelope.

Key Developments

Theatre 01

Russia – Ukraine

UGV Route-Clearance & EW Interdiction around Lyman
ManoeuvreFires & EW
Ukraine isolates Russian infiltration forces around Lyman

Ukraine adapted Operation Vivaldi around Lyman by targeting the reinforcement and supply links supporting Russian infiltration groups rather than immediately clearing each forward position. Ukrainian forces combined UGV route-clearance, EW, strikes on drone teams and command posts, and interdiction of ATV- and motorcycle-borne reinforcements to isolate Russian troops north and northwest of Lyman before clearing them. The approach demonstrates how limited manoeuvre can be restored in a drone-saturated battlespace by breaking the adversary’s ISR–C2–logistics chain.isw

Implication

Small infiltration groups become operationally ineffective if their resupply, reinforcement and casualty-evacuation routes are interdicted. Prioritise fires, UAS and EW against mobility corridors, rather than committing infantry immediately to every forward clearance fight.

Ukrainian UGVs reportedly demined 30 km of routes in two nights toward Shandryholove. This illustrates how robotic breaching and route-clearance systems can restore movement tempo while reducing sapper exposure in mine- and drone-threatened terrain.

Theatre 02

Israel – Lebanon

Transition to an Enduring Layered Security Zone
ManoeuvreArea Security
Israel transitions to enduring security-zone control in southern Lebanon

Israel’s most significant operational development this week was the transition from clearance operations to a more permanent, layered security-zone posture in southern Lebanon. The IDF reportedly began reorganising forces within the "Yellow Zone", reducing and redeploying troops while establishing a new line of forward positions for an extended deployment. Hezbollah’s attempted explosive-drone attack on Israeli troops and Israel’s subsequent strikes on suspected drone-launch and anti-tank missile sites illustrate the evolving contest: Israel is seeking to hold key terrain and movement corridors with a more sustainable footprint, while Hezbollah is relying on dispersed UAS, anti-tank and covert cells to impose force-protection costs and challenge Israeli freedom of movement.anadolu

Implication

A forward line alone is vulnerable to UAS, ATGM and IED attack. Enduring control requires mutually supporting posts, protected logistics routes, reserve/reaction forces, persistent ISR and the ability to strike beyond the immediate line of contact.

New positions must be built for drone- and ATGM-dominated warfare: overhead protection, dispersed shelters, decoys, hardened C2, concealed vehicle parks and rapid route repair are essential from the outset, not later improvements.

Theatre 03

Israel – Gaza

Counter-Reconstitution: Tunnel Demolition & Finance-Node Targeting
Manoeuvre
Shift toward a persistent, limited-security-zone campaign rather than a large manoeuvre offensive

The key operational development was the IDF’s continued transition to an enduring counter-reconstitution posture inside and around the Gaza security-control area, illustrated by the reported demolition of a 2 km underground tunnel in southern Gaza’s "Yellow Line" and a precision strike that Israel says killed Hamas finance department head Muhammad Abu Alwan in Khan Younis. Rather than signalling a return to broad, division-level urban clearance, these actions suggest a campaign focused on denying Hamas the means to regenerate: degrading remaining subterranean infrastructure, maintaining access and surveillance over selected terrain, and selectively targeting leadership and financial nodes. The development also underlines that a ceasefire or reduced-intensity framework does not end ground-force requirements; it shifts them toward persistent ISR, protected mobility, route/tunnel exploitation and intelligence-led raids.calibergov.il

Implication

Land forces must transition mentally and doctrinally from large-scale combat operations (LSCO) focused on "clear and hold" to continuous counter-reconstitution operations. The primary objective shifts from territorial conquest to denying the enemy the capacity to reorganise, re-arm, and rebuild infrastructure.

Achieving effective counter-reconstitution demands an unblinking ISR umbrella over the operational area. Ground units become sensory nodes feeding real-time targeting cycles, requiring seamless fusion between tactical ground patrols, overhead UAS, and financial/human intelligence (FININT/HUMINT).

Theatre 04

Israel – US – Iran

Dual-Chokepoint A2/AD: Hormuz Blockade & Bab al-Mandeb Consolidation
Operational Development
Maritime chokepoint escalation and nuclear-infrastructure targeting posture

Trilateral tensions across the Israel–US–Iran axis were characterised by escalation along key maritime chokepoints and targeting posture surrounding Iran's nuclear infrastructure. Iran expanded efforts to enforce a transit blockade in the Strait of Hormuz through asymmetric maritime harassment while utilising domestic and proxy subterranean fortification — notably expanding underground works at Natanz and along Yemen's Bab al-Mandeb coast alongside IRGC advisers. Concurrently, US naval enforcement against Iranian ports forced Tehran to rely on alternative air supply routes for missile components. In response to Iranian nuclear facility reinforcement, Israeli leadership reaffirmed readiness to execute unilateral or coalition precision strikes, signalling that regional operational pauses remain temporary while strategic red lines are tested.timesofisrael

Houthi & Regional Proxies
Red Sea interdiction and Saudi escalation

Operational developments among Iran’s regional proxies were dominated by the Houthi movement consolidating territorial and operational control over Yemen’s west coast and the vital Bab al-Mandeb shipping corridor. Following the seizure of Mokha and Mayun Island, Houthi forces blunted government counter-offensives around key heights in Lahj, firmly locking down the Red Sea choke point. Concurrently, the Houthis escalated long-range fires into Saudi Arabia, launching ballistic missiles and sUAS swarms targeting Riyadh and southern defence sites, drawing heavy Saudi air strikes against Houthi military sites in Taiz. Supported by embedded IRGC technical advisers, Houthi coastal anti-access/area-denial (A2/AD) capabilities now directly complement Iranian maritime interdiction efforts across the wider Middle Eastern maritime domain.koreaherald

Implication

The simultaneous threat to the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab al-Mandeb Strait establishes a coordinated, multi-theatre A2/AD envelope. Ground forces can no longer rely on secure maritime logistics in littoral zones. Expeditionary army units operating in or near these theatres must field organic, shore-based anti-ship cruise missiles (ASCMs), mobile coastal radars, and amphibious breaching capabilities to clear or secure critical choke points.

Operating against heavily fortified or time-sensitive targets requires compressing the sensor-to-shooter cycle. Tactical army units must seamlessly fuse real-time overhead ISR with ground-based precision fires to exploit brief windows of exposure when forces emerge from subterranean complexes.

Theatre 05

Thailand – Cambodia

Combat-Readiness Order at Mounts 225/350 and Ta Kwai
Strategic
Heightened contingency readiness at sensitive border terrain

The most significant development during 21–28 September was the Thai Army’s order for troops at key Thai–Cambodian border locations — including Mounts 225 and 350 and the Ta Kwai temple area — to maintain combat readiness. The order indicates that Bangkok is sustaining a heightened contingency posture around tactically and politically sensitive terrain, where small-scale incidents involving patrols, fortifications, mines or unmanned systems could escalate rapidly. This more likely reflects preparation for localised border contingencies and force-protection requirements than an immediate indication of planned large-scale cross-border operations.thaipbsworld

Implication

A ceasefire environment may still demand combat-ready forward forces. Units require rehearsed transition drills from monitoring and presence patrols to local defence, protected reinforcement and controlled counteraction.

Standing defensive positions on key terrain are primary targets for preemptive or retaliatory mortar salvos. Command nodes, supply caches, and troop staging areas must remain dispersed, camouflaged, and mobile to reduce signatures and avoid single-point destruction from localised indirect fire.
