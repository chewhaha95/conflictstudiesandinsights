20 July – 27 July 2026

BLUF — Bottom Line Up Front

Across all five theatres, operations are transitioning from open kinetic manoeuvre toward strategic interdiction, third-party buffer management, and physical terrain anchoring. While Ukraine shifts to asymmetric long-range strikes against Russian maritime supply routes amidst leadership restructuring, the Middle Eastern and Southeast Asian theatres are hardening perimeter control — leveraging Lebanese and international stabilisation contingents, US–Iran maritime negotiation lulls, and reinforced border engineering to cement tactical positions.

Key Developments

Theatre 01

Russia – Ukraine

Strategic Interdiction + Deep Command Reshuffle
Fires & StrikesC2
Strategic interdiction and deep command reshuffle

The week was defined by Ukraine's intensive long-range air and maritime interdiction campaign against Russian economic arteries, alongside major internal structural friction in Kyiv. Ukrainian drone strikes targeted Russian shipping and oil terminals in the Sea of Azov and Black Sea — striking over 100 vessels and forcing a halt to Russian grain and fuel transits — while deep strikes hit infrastructure as far as the Urals. Concurrently, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy executed a major military leadership overhaul. Following Fedorov's initial removal on 15 July, nationwide public backlash and military unrest forced President Zelenskyy to replace Gen. Syrskyi on 21 July with Maj. Gen. Mykhailo Drapatyi — a respected general who aligns with Fedorov's drone-first, modernising vision.moscowtimes

Manoeuvre
Tactical ground dynamics: the "kill zone" gridlock

Ground operations remained dominated by localised, high-attrition infantry infiltration rather than large-scale armoured manoeuvre. Tactical progress across major sectors (Pokrovsk, Toretsk, and Chasiv Yar) yielded only incremental territory shifts. Both forces maintained a high-density, ~40 km-wide uncrewed aerial systems (UAS) "kill zone" along the frontline. Reconnaissance and FPV drones severely restricted armoured mobility, forcing both sides to rely heavily on small-unit infantry assault groups, dismounted logistics, and uncrewed ground vehicles (UGVs) for localised resupply.dignitasreuters

Implication

Operational success is increasingly decoupled from near-FLOT manoeuvres; long-range asymmetric fires targeting an adversary's economic-logistical spine can generate theatre-level operational paralysis faster than attrition on the front lines. The appointment of Drapatyi represents a shift toward asymmetric multi-domain force development — combining near-FLOT robotic/drone gridlocks with deep strike campaigns — rather than an isolated shift to long-range fires. Deep interdiction serves as the operational fires lever within this broader reform, enabling Ukraine to degrade Russian logistics while preserving infantry combat power.

Theatre 02

Israel – Lebanon

Pilot Zone Handover Begins — With Caveats
Protection
Trilateral pilot implementation: Israel, US, Lebanese "Pilot Zones" deployment

On 20 July, Israel, the US, and Lebanese authorities announced the formal launch of a pilot programme for designated safe/security zones in southern Lebanon under the US-brokered framework agreement signed in late June. In line with the agreement, the Lebanese Armed Forces (LAF) began forward deployment into designated pilot zones — notably entering Zawtar al-Gharbiyeh, alongside operations near Froun and Srifa, following partial Israeli posture realignments. The objective is phased Hezbollah disarmament and state authority re-assertion in areas south of the Litani River. The Israeli Air Force (IAF) maintained a strict "Active Enforcement" doctrine, conducting localised precision strikes against suspected Hezbollah arms reconstitutions — with the LAF publicly reporting that IDF strikes hit their own positions. Hezbollah maintained a low profile near the Blue Line, using political leverage in Beirut to resist formal disarmament mandates while covertly preserving subterranean networks north of the Litani.al-monitortimesofisraeltimesofisrael

Implication [Operational Buffer]

Relying on a third-party conventional force (the LAF) as an operational buffer against an embedded non-state actor creates persistent tactical friction. When the proxy force lacks the political mandate or capability to forcibly disarm insurgents, the primary military power is forced to maintain a permanent standoff posture with frequent kinetic "interventions", preventing true operational end-state transition.

Theatre 03

Israel – Gaza

Cabinet Approves International Stabilisation Force
CoalitionC2
Cabinet approval of International Security Framework

On 26 July, the Israeli Security Cabinet officially approved the legal framework permitting an International Stabilisation Force (ISF) to enter Gaza, clearing the way for foreign contingents (including troops from Morocco and Uganda) to deploy to areas outside IDF control. Israel maintained strict operational caveats: every foreign contingent requires specific Israeli authorisation, and ISF personnel are restricted from operating within the Israeli-occupied buffer zone east of the designated separation boundaries. This political-military shift follows weeks of high-tempo Israeli zone-enforcement strikes against Hamas commanders and weapons sites, alongside Hamas's administrative dissolution, which intentionally created a governance vacuum.straitstimes

Implication

The transition from active counter-insurgency to a multi-national security framework introduces complex C2 and coalition deconfliction challenges. Conventional forces operating alongside disparate international contingents must establish rigid, real-time ROE and airspace management protocols to prevent friendly-fire incidents while preventing non-state insurgents from exploiting gaps between international sectors.

Theatre 04

Israel – US – Iran

US Refuellers Forward-Based; 13-Night Strike Pause
Sustainment
US mass tanker forward-basing & air mobility staging in Israel

Early in the week (around 20 July), the US military executed a massive forward deployment of aerial refuelling assets (KC-135s and KC-46s) into Israeli installations, specifically Ramon Air Base in southern Israel and Ben Gurion Airport. The build-up was designed to extend the loiter time and strike radius of US combat aircraft operating over Iran and the Persian Gulf without relying solely on Gulf state airbases. Crucially, Washington used this logistical posture to maintain unilateral strike capacity while actively working to keep the Israeli Air Force (IAF) out of direct offensive sorties inside Iran to prevent immediate regional retaliation against Israeli urban centres.misbar

Strategic Development
Tactical pause in escalation following two-week exchange

Following 13 consecutive nights of heavy US CENTCOM airstrikes against Iranian coastal defences and IRGC regional sites, fighting temporarily paused over the weekend of 25–26 July. The pause came after intense Iranian ballistic missile and drone counter-offensives across the Gulf, which heavily impacted early-warning arrays, regional maritime traffic, and US logistics nodes. Both sides paused kinetic operations to allow US–Iran diplomatic envoys and Omani mediators space to negotiate maritime access protocols through the Strait of Hormuz.reuters

Implication

Modern high-intensity regional air/missile wars consume interceptor inventories and disrupt early-warning/ISR architectures at an unsustainable rate, making operational pauses a logistical necessity for both sides. Land forces forward-deployed within the threat envelope must utilise operational lulls to aggressively reset passive protection, disperse command nodes, and resupply critical air defence magazines before kinetic exchanges resume.

Theatre 05

Thailand – Cambodia

1.3 km Fence Completed; 7 km Expansion Coming
ManoeuvreCombat Engineering
Border fence rollout at Chanthaburi province

On 22 July, the Royal Thai Armed Forces, led by Chief of Defence Forces Gen. Ukrit Boontanon, unveiled the completion of a 1.3-kilometre reinforced pilot border fence between border markers 52 and 54 in the Pong Nam Ron district of Chanthaburi province. Built using heavy concrete panels topped with steel mesh and concertina wire, the barrier was explicitly tested and confirmed to resist small-arms fire. The Thai military confirmed plans to immediately expand this into a 7-kilometre second phase (markers 54 to 59) to establish permanent physical access control and territorial anchoring along contested operational sectors.bangkokpost

Implication [Manoeuvre]

Combat engineering assets act as force multipliers during fragile ceasefires by permanently altering physical terrain and establishing new defensive realities without crossing kinetic thresholds. Once an army hardens new tactical high ground with permanent engineering barriers, the operational burden shifts entirely to the opposing force, who must either accept the loss of terrain or initiate open, high-risk offensive actions to breach fortified positions.
