BLUF — Bottom Line Up Front

Ukraine has launched a targeted 40-day campaign against Russian infrastructure and successfully scaled cheap FPV drones to intercept Russian Shahed drones. In the Middle East, Israel continues strikes within its southern Lebanon security zone despite a newly signed trilateral peace agreement, as Hamas heavily suppressed a rare, organised civilian uprising at 18 locations across Gaza. Simultaneously, intense US–Iran maritime clashes and deep regional IRGC missile strikes against US bases forced a fragile "stand-down" agreement alongside a massive surge in Iranian cyberattacks against Israel. Finally, in Southeast Asia, Thailand's night-time F-16 flights along the border have triggered diplomatic protests from Cambodia, highlighting the growing use of routine military manoeuvres for deliberate strategic signalling.

Key Developments

Theatre 01

Russia – Ukraine

40-Day Campaign & FPV-as-Interceptor
ManoeuvreInformation Operations
Ukraine announces 40-day campaign to force Russia to end the war; Donetsk front static

Zelenskyy publicly announced on 25 June that he authorised a 40-day operation to "influence the aggressor state to push for an end to the conflict". The campaign is targeting Russian energy infrastructure, Crimea supply routes, and logistics hubs. On the ground, Russian forces continued slow advances toward Kostiantynivka while the frontline in Donetsk remained broadly static. ISW assessed on 26 June that Ukraine's mid-range strikes are already affecting Russian logistics across occupied territories. Russia's summer advance in Donetsk has failed to meet its intended objectives; ISW assessed it is "no longer prepared to forecast when Russian forces might seize Donetsk".guardianisw

Fires & Strikes
FPV drones confirmed as Shahed interceptors; production-chain strikes continue

Ukrainian forces used FPV drones to intercept Russian Shahed drones over Crimea during the week — confirming FPV-as-interceptor as a repeatable, scalable counter-UAS tactic. Ukraine also struck the Titan-Barrikady ballistic missile development and production plant with an FP-5 Flamingo cruise missile — targeting the factory that produces the missiles used to strike Ukrainian cities. Ukraine's Fire Point signed an agreement with Germany's Hensoldt radar company to develop a European missile defence interceptor using the FP-7X rocket and TRML-4D radar.guardian

Implication [Manoeuvre · Information Operations]

Zelenskyy's public announcement of a named campaign with a defined duration is deliberate strategic communication. Planners should note how Ukraine is using the timing and public framing of military campaigns as a diplomatic pressure tool — setting a political clock, signalling resolve to Western backers, and forcing Russia to respond publicly. This is a model for how a smaller military can use campaign announcements to shape the political environment around an operation.

Implication [Fires & Strikes]
  • Production-chain targeting is more cost-effective than per-missile interception — hitting the production facility is more cost-effective than intercepting each missile it produces. Fires and targeting planners should think about nodes in the production chain, not just the delivered weapon.
  • FPV drones as interceptors are now confirmed — cheap, scalable, and do not deplete expensive interceptor stocks. C-UAS planning should include this as a low-cost layer.
  • Combat-proven systems integrating with European-standard sensors — Ukraine's Fire Point–Hensoldt partnership shows combat-tested platforms are being adapted to NATO-standard sensors. Procurement planners should assess these as potential options before European demand absorbs production capacity.
Theatre 02

Israel – Lebanon

14-Point Framework Signed; IDF Strikes Hours Later
ManoeuvreDiplomacy
IDF strikes Lebanon hours after signing; security zone maintained

Lebanon, Israel, and the US signed a 14-point trilateral framework agreement in Washington on 26 June after five rounds of talks. Secretary of State Rubio called it "the beginning of the beginning". The framework creates two pilot zones where Lebanese army forces will take over from IDF, linked to phased Hezbollah disarmament — but it provides no timeline for full Israeli withdrawal and no mechanism for forcing Hezbollah compliance.lemondestraitstimes

Israel launched a drone strike in southern Lebanon on 27 June — the day after the framework was signed. Further strikes were reported on 28 June, with Lebanon's NNA documenting multiple strike locations. Israel stated the strikes targeted Hezbollah operatives within its declared 10 km security zone. Netanyahu confirmed Israeli forces will remain in the security zone "until Hezbollah is disarmed".reuters

Implication [CIMIC · Force Protection · C2]

The framework creates a role for an international Military Coordination Group for Lebanon — a body in which regional partners may be asked to participate. Planners should begin preliminary scoping for what a contribution to such a body would require: ROE for operating alongside both IDF (in its security zone) and Lebanese army (trying to enforce the framework), force protection in a zone where Hezbollah operates freely and has declared it will continue resistance, and C2 under a US-led trilateral structure. The Lebanese army's position — asked to enforce an agreement the primary armed actor has rejected, without the tools to do it — is the risk template planners must work around.

Theatre 03

Israel – Gaza

"June 26 Revolution" Suppressed at 18 Sites
Civil-MilitaryCIMIC
"June 26 Revolution" planned at 18 sites; Hamas suppresses with hospital-based control nodes

Palestinian activists organised the "June 26 Revolution" — also called "Day of Rage" and "We Want to Live" — planning protests at 18 locations across Gaza. Hamas deployed operatives, al-Qassam Brigade militias, and intelligence units across the Strip to prevent gatherings. Hamas turned four hospitals — Al-Shifa, Nasser, Al-Ahli Arab, and Al-Aqsa Martyrs — into "makeshift police stations, interrogation sites, and torture centres". The Association of Palestinian Scholars issued a fatwa warning protesters they would be classified as Israeli collaborators, punishable by death under Hamas rule. Streets were "eerily empty" on 26 June; a small number of protesters appeared in Gaza City and Khan Younis despite the crackdown.timesofisrael

Implication [Civil-Military · Information Operations]

The "June 26 Revolution" is strategically significant even though it was suppressed: it confirms that organised civilian opposition to Hamas exists inside Gaza, is growing, and has now taken a public, named form. For CIMIC planners, this is relevant to any future stabilisation scenario. The population the stabilisation force would serve is not a homogeneous pro-Hamas bloc — there is a visible internal constituency that wants Hamas removed. Planning for civil-military relations in Gaza must account for this divide, not treat Gaza as a single population bloc.

Theatre 04

Israel – US – Iran

Maritime Clashes, IRGC Base Strikes, Cyber Surge
Fires & StrikesMaritime
Tit-for-tat strikes in the Gulf before 29 June stand-down

Despite an interim peace framework, intense tit-for-tat strikes erupted in the Persian Gulf and surrounding areas before a fresh "stand-down" agreement was reached on 28–29 June.

Maritime sabotage & air retaliation: On 25 June, an Iranian drone struck a foreign-flagged cargo ship off Oman, followed by a strike on the Panama-flagged M/T Kiku on 27 June. In response, US military aircraft conducted heavy retaliatory strikes against Iranian surveillance infrastructure, air defence sites, and drone/missile storage facilities on Qeshm Island and along the strait.maritime-executiveisw

Deep regional strikes: Escalating further, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) launched ballistic missile and drone attacks targeting the US Fifth Fleet Naval Base in Bahrain and the Ali Al Salem Airbase in Kuwait on 28 June.timesofisrael

The 29 June de-escalation: Following intense backchannel communication, senior US officials confirmed on Monday morning (29 June) that both sides have agreed to "stop all kinetic activity" and stand down for now, allowing commercial vessels to resume free movement.thehindu

Cyber Domain
Cyber domain expansion: 4,800 incidents in a single month

Beyond kinetic attacks, the shadow war saw a massive spike in digital operations. Israel's National Cyber Directorate reported on 29 June that Iranian cyberattacks against Israeli target networks have surged dramatically, registering approximately 4,800 hostile cyber incidents over the month — a threefold increase compared to the same period last year.reuterscna

Implication [C4 & Digitalisation Command]

A ceasefire on the ground does not equal a ceasefire in cyberspace. 4,800 incidents in a single month is a volume-saturation strategy. Not all 4,800 are sophisticated attacks — many will be noise, probing, phishing, or low-sophistication intrusion attempts. The intent is to overwhelm defensive capacity and create analyst fatigue so that the genuinely dangerous intrusions are harder to spot. If human analysts try to manually investigate every single event, analyst fatigue sets in within days. AI must handle the automated filtering of 95% of the "noise" so that human expertise is strictly reserved for the remaining 5% of highly anomalous, sophisticated threats.

Theatre 05

Thailand – Cambodia

Night F-16 Flights as Below-Threshold Signalling
Information Operations
RTAF issues formal clarification on F-16 night flights; Cambodia characterises them as military signalling

The Royal Thai Air Force issued a formal public clarification on 22 June following social media attention on significant F-16 activity along the Thai–Cambodian border overnight. The RTAF confirmed the flights were part of standard monthly night-training exercises to maintain round-the-clock readiness, that all aircraft remained within Thai sovereign airspace, and that the exercises would continue as part of routine border surveillance. PM Anutin confirmed the same morning: "it was a drill". The RTAF framed public awareness as "a positive demonstration of national defence capabilities" — a dual signal that the training justification was genuine and the strategic signalling was intentional. Cambodia characterised the flights as military sabre-rattling and lodged a diplomatic observation.thailandnews

Implication [Emerging Tactics · Information Operations]

Thailand is using routine military activity as calibrated below-threshold signalling — legally defensible, deniable, and message-carrying. The risk is miscalculation: if Cambodia observes flights it cannot authenticate as training, it may escalate on the basis of misperception. The RTAF's next-morning clarification was the right response but came after a full night of unaddressed speculation. Pre-emptive public communication before or immediately after a signalling exercise — not reactive clarification — is the better practice for units conducting operations with signalling value near contested borders or in sensitive environments.
